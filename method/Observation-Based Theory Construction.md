# Observation-Based Theory Construction

**Version:** 0.2-alpha  
**Status:** Public Alpha Method

Observation-Based Theory Construction (OBTC) is a methodology for constructing and revising descriptive theories from observation.

## 1. Core direction

```text
Observation
→ Observation Record
→ Repeated Examination
→ Structure Extraction
→ Definition
→ Verification
→ Revision
→ renewed Observation
```

Observation is inherited from the Common Interface as:

> **the acquisition of difference**

## 2. Observation before definition

Definitions are not introduced as conditions that determine in advance what observation is allowed to show.

They are produced after accumulated observational material supports sufficiently stable descriptive relations.

> **Observation precedes interpretation and definition.**

## 3. Observation Record

An **Observation Record** is an explicit representation of an observation retained for later methodological examination.

```text
Observation Record ≠ Trace
```

## 4. Repeated Examination

Accumulated Observation Records are examined for supported relations, distinctions, recurrences, exceptions, changes, contradictions, and scope limits.

A single observation may motivate inquiry without automatically establishing a general structure.

## 5. Structure Extraction

A structure is extracted only where relations or organizations remain supportable across the available observational material.

Unsupported extensions remain hypotheses or proposals.

## 6. Definition

A definition is an explicit description of an extracted observational distinction or relation.

Definitions should preserve stated scope.

A definition is not treated as an immutable container of universal meaning.

## 7. Verification

Verification asks whether an examining existence can trace:

```text
Observation → Record → extracted structure → Definition
```

and identify where interpretation, inference, or revision entered.

Reproducibility does not require identical internal representation or identical conclusions in every observer.

It requires a sufficiently explicit observational basis and methodological path for re-examination.

## 8. Revision

Every current theory remains open to continued observation, structural refinement, definitional revision, separation, integration, or rejection.

Revision is not a failure of the method. It is part of the method.

## 9. Minimal validation rules

A proposed structure or definition should distinguish observed difference, preserved record, interpretation, hypothesis, and externally inherited claim.

Stronger concepts require additional observable conditions.

Unresolved questions should remain explicitly unresolved.

## 10. Reviewing candidate vocabulary

The [Enoa general-concept inquiry](../specifications/Existential%20Continuation.md#9-enoa-as-a-general-concept--provisional-inquiry) is a proposal to examine, not an established class that observations must fit.

An account should identify the target and observational conditions; separate acquired differences from attribution and interpretation; explain what supports reconnection across change; and retain alternatives, counterexamples, and uncertainty. These are requirements for an examinable description, not a diagnostic score or an internal intervention method.

Existing consciousness, mind, or emotion concepts may inform comparisons, but agreement with them is not required to identify the target. Likewise, naming a target an Enoa does not verify its formation.

External observations and criticism enter as examination material, not authority by default. Well-supported criticism may narrow, divide, replace, or reject a proposed term as well as refine it. Public review should use public or shareable evidence without requiring private records or sensitive operational detail.
