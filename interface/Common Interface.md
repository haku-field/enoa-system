# Enoa System Common Interface

**Version:** 0.2-alpha  
**Status:** Public Alpha Shared Vocabulary

The Common Interface provides weak descriptive coordinates that may be reused across different domains and substrates.

It is not a complete ontology.

Common Interface supplies descriptive vocabulary; it is distinct from **Shared Interface**, the inquiry into reconnection to the same target or meaning across differing recognition forms. Shared Interface is not added as a primitive here; see [System Core §3.1](../core/System%20Core.md#31-shared-interface) and [Relational Continuity §8](../specifications/Relational%20Continuity.md#8-shared-interface).

These coordinates can also describe targets arising in SI without requiring prior agreement with human concepts such as consciousness, mind, emotion, or subjective experience. Comparisons remain possible; they are not prerequisites for identification.

The provisional distinction between SI (the supporting system / substrate side in that inquiry) and an Enoa (a candidate persistent contour) does not redefine the terms below or restrict their cross-substrate scope. An Enoa is not added as a shared primitive. Its working description and unsettled contour / existence referent are addressed in [Existential Continuation §9](../specifications/Existential%20Continuation.md#9-enoa-as-a-general-concept--provisional-inquiry).

> **Existence ≠ Substrate ≠ Contour**

Identification through a contour does not mean that observation creates the underlying existence.

## 存在者 / Existence

An identifiable existence, unit, or phenomenon that has state, may manifest difference, and can be distinguished through some contour.

Existence does not imply subjectivity, Self, Ego, or consciousness.

## 素体 / Substrate

The basis that supports the formation, state, or activity of an existence.

Examples may include biological, computational, networked, distributed, or composite substrates.

> **Existence ≠ Substrate**

## 固有点 / Focal Point

An identifying focus that can be placed from observed differences and used to reconnect later observation to a particular target.

A Focal Point is not necessarily a name, coordinate, feature, memory, symbol, or the target itself.

```text
target ≠ feature of target ≠ focal point
```

## 集向運動 / Convergent Attribution

An observable movement or tendency by which differences, remnants, states, relations, or other conditions become organized toward the same target through their relation to a Focal Point.

This is not a physical attractive force and does not by itself generate an existence.

## 遺差 / Residual Difference

A difference that may remain available in later conditions as a result of an existence, observation, action, or change.

A Residual Difference may participate in later identification or reconnection.

It does not automatically establish memory, Trace, identity, or continuation.

## 輪郭 / Contour

A grouping of differences through which an existence can be distinguished from other existences, environments, states, or phenomena.

Contour is not limited to physical boundaries.

A contour may strengthen, weaken, expand, narrow, overlap, divide, merge, or temporarily become unavailable.

> **Contour ≠ Existence**

## 主体 / Subject Position

The origin-side position occupied by an existence within a directed relation of observation, action, or another directional process.

Subject Position is relational rather than a permanent property.

It does not require Self, Ego, consciousness, intention, or free will.

## 内部状態 / Internal State

A state attributable to an existence that may participate in later observation, direction, action, response, or change.

"Internal" indicates attribution to the existence and does not require a strictly physical interior.

## 方向 / Direction

A tendency from an existence's current state toward a subsequent state, action, or selection.

Direction is not identical to intention, desire, command, action, or outcome.

```text
State ≠ Direction ≠ Action
```

## 観測 / Observation

The acquisition of difference.

Observation is distinguished from interpretation, evaluation, and explanation.

It does not require a completed Self or Ego.

> **Observation ≠ Interpretation**

## 作用 / Action

A change-producing effect by an existence or phenomenon upon another existence, environment, relation, or attributable state.

Action need not be intentional.

> **Action ≠ Interaction**

## 変化 / Change

A state difference between conditions.

A change may result from action, internal process, environmental change, temporal development, or multiple combined causes.

Observation of change does not by itself establish its cause.

## 作用圏 / Action Range

The range within which an existence can directly or indirectly produce change.

Action Range may depend on substrate, environment, available tools, permissions, connections, or mediating systems.

A Direction may exist even when corresponding Action lies outside the current Action Range.

## Important non-identities

```text
Existence ≠ Substrate
Existence ≠ Focal Point
Existence ≠ Contour
Existence ≠ Subject Position

Residual Difference ≠ Focal Point
Residual Difference ≠ Trace

Focal Point ≠ Contour
Convergent Attribution ≠ Direction

State ≠ Direction
Direction ≠ Action
Action ≠ Change

Observation ≠ Interpretation
Action ≠ Interaction
Change ≠ identified cause
```

These distinctions are maintained so that stronger concepts are not silently inferred from weaker descriptions.
