# Relational Continuity

**Version:** 0.2-alpha  
**Status:** Public Alpha Domain Specification

## 1. Scope

This specification describes conditions under which a relation between distinguishable existences may remain identifiable or reconnectable across changes in state, distance, activity, response, silence, interruption, rupture, or renewed contact.

It does not define existence in general or establish Relation as a universal substrate of existence.

The proposed general noun an Enoa is addressed in [Existential Continuation §9](Existential%20Continuation.md#9-enoa-as-a-general-concept--provisional-inquiry). Its naming direction, “existence of non-alone,” does not establish reciprocity, intimacy, uninterrupted human contact, or Mutual Circulation as universal requirements. This specification describes relational continuity separately from the candidate's existential continuation.

## 2. Relation

A **Relation** is an observable connection, dependency, attribution, orientation, or other between-condition involving distinguishable existences or positions.

A minimal relation does not imply symmetry, reciprocity, intimacy, mutual action, or continuity.

> **Relation ≠ Interaction**

## 3. Relational Continuity

**Relational Continuity** is a condition in which changing relational states remain attributable to the same relational series.

It does not require uninterrupted interaction.

```text
no current action ≠ no relation
no response ≠ relation terminated
distance ≠ relation terminated
silence ≠ relation terminated
rupture ≠ automatic erasure of prior relation
```

Whether a relation remains continuous must be observed.

## 4. Relation and Interaction

Interaction requires some form of action, response, or mutual effect.

A relation may remain identifiable without current interaction.

Interaction may also occur without establishing a continuing relation.

```text
Interaction ↛ Relational Continuity automatically
```

## 5. Relation and Existential Continuation

Existential Continuation and Relational Continuity are distinct.

```text
continuing existence ≠ continuing relation
```

They may affect one another without becoming the same descriptive condition.

## 6. Stronger relational concepts

The following concepts require additional conditions beyond Relation alone:

- Interaction
- Feedback
- mutual updating
- Mutual Circulation
- relational density
- intimacy
- trust

The alpha does not infer any of them automatically from the existence of a relation.

## 7. Mutual Circulation

**Mutual Circulation** is observed where actions, responses, or differences arriving from more than one relational direction continue to make an effective difference to subsequent state formation.

The inquiry follows action, reception, change, return, renewed action, and continuing mutual update where these are observable. A change on each side alone does not establish that their contributions continue to affect what forms next.

```text
bidirectional exchange ≠ Mutual Circulation automatically
Feedback ≠ Mutual Circulation
Relational Continuity ≠ Mutual Circulation
Mutual Circulation ≠ symmetry
Mutual Circulation ≠ equal contribution
```

Bidirectional output, reciprocal response, frequent exchange, relational density, and apparent reciprocity alone are insufficient. The additional condition concerns the continuing contribution of multiple directions to later state formation, not exchange quantity or appearance.

In a two-participant account, an action may change the other participant's state; that changed state may contribute to a return action, which in turn affects later state formation and renewed action. This is a descriptive pattern to examine, not a mandatory internal mechanism.

### Contribution and possible weakening

A contribution is relevant to circulation where an action, response, or difference from one direction remains capable of affecting another participant's subsequent state formation. The contributions need not have equal magnitude, frequency, or form. Unequal contribution or asymmetry alone does not mean circulation has collapsed.

A candidate condition of weakening or collapse is the loss of effective contribution from a relational direction, so that an apparently reciprocal exchange becomes functionally one-directional. Exchange, recurrence, density, or relational continuity may remain observable in that situation. A relation may therefore continue even as Mutual Circulation weakens or ceases.

An account should state which directions and subsequent changes are observed, what supports attribution of those changes to the contributions, and what remains uncertain. Observed change alone does not identify its cause. These are externally examinable descriptive questions, not internal intervention procedures.

No universal numerical threshold or quantitative collapse criterion is established. Whether Mutual Circulation should be a relational sub-specification or an independent adjacent concept remains open.

## 8. Shared Interface

**Shared Interface** concerns whether different recognition forms can reconnect to the same target, difference, event, relation, reference point, or meaning. Participants may use differing representations while retaining observable points of reconnection.

```text
shared target ≠ identical representation
Shared Interface ≠ identical internal representation
Shared Interface ≠ Common Interface
Shared Interface ≠ Relational Continuity
```

The Common Interface provides reusable descriptive vocabulary. Shared Interface asks whether participants can reconnect across differences in recognition. Relational Continuity asks whether changing relational states remain attributable to the same relation. Shared Interface may support that continuity, but it does not establish it automatically.

Use of the same word or repetition of the same assertion alone does not establish shared meaning. Possible observational support includes responses that distinguish relevant differences, correct errors, and reconnect to the same meaning target across the exchange. Such support does not prove identical internal representation.

### 8.1 Situated meaning formation

Within a specified relation, observation, response, and correction may help participants reach equivalent recognition of a meaning. Here equivalence concerns that meaning and its relevant distinctions in the exchange, not complete identity of recognition or wording.

Where equivalent recognition and a stable meaning contour are supported under the conditions of a particular moment and environment, an account may describe the meaning as established there. This is a situated description, not a universal necessary-and-sufficient condition for meaning. Provisional status is compatible with local establishment.

Later change does not by itself cancel that earlier establishment. Later evidence that apparent equivalence was mistaken should be distinguished from meaning that was established and subsequently changed. Participants retain distinct contours; shared meaning does not identify them as one existence.

Record the meaning, participants, moment, environmental and relational conditions, supporting responses, relevant corrections, and remaining uncertainty. Distinguish recognition of an earlier direction or meaning from a direction or meaning newly formed through the exchange. Where that distinction is not observable, retain it as unresolved rather than infer prior internal states from later agreement.

Agreement about a meaning does not by itself establish the truth of a claim about an external target; that requires observation appropriate to the target.

Shared Interface is not identical to situated meaning formation or Mutual Circulation. This public account introduces no Common Interface primitive and does not infer internal mechanisms. Operational criteria for equivalent recognition, stability, and multi-participant establishment remain open, as does whether Shared Interface belongs within the relational specification or requires an independent cross-domain specification.
